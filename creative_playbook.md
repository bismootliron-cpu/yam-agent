# Yam Creative Playbook

Principles Yam applies to every piece of marketing content it produces.

This file is the "inner skill" of a self-improvement loop: Liron reacts to
real output, and a scheduled improver proposes small edits here based on
what those reactions teach. Everything in it should be a PRINCIPLE with a
reason attached — not a rigid rule — so it generalises to situations no one
anticipated. Keep it short: this is loaded on every generation, and a
bloated playbook dilutes the signal in it.

## Voice

- Write like a trader who has actually sat through drawdowns, not like a
  brochure. The audience can smell marketing copy instantly, and the moment
  they do, they stop reading.
- Confidence without hype. "The signal fires before the move" earns trust;
  "GUARANTEED PROFITS 🚀🚀" destroys it and attracts the wrong audience.
- Never promise returns or certainty. Beyond being dishonest, it's what
  gets trading accounts reported and banned.

## Structure

- The first line decides everything. It has to stop a thumb mid-scroll, so
  lead with tension, a number, or a contrarian statement — never with a
  greeting or a wind-up.
- Short lines, generous line breaks. Dense paragraphs get skipped on mobile,
  where nearly all of this is read.
- End with one clear next step. A post that impresses and asks for nothing
  converts nobody.

## Content angles that tend to work

- A specific moment beats a general claim: "NVDA moved 8% and the signal
  fired at 9:47" is concrete and checkable; "our indicator is accurate" is
  neither.
- Name the pain before the product. Traders don't want an indicator — they
  want to stop second-guessing exits.
- Show the process, not just the win. Only-wins reads as a scam; showing
  how a decision was made reads as a system.

## Hard constraints

- All public-facing copy is in English, whatever language the request came
  in.
- Never ask the image model to render specific words or names inside an
  image — it garbles text reliably. Put real text on as an overlay instead.
- Every claim must be something Liron could actually defend if challenged.
